## Besant Nagar Ashtalakshmi Kovil

Located on Chennai's coastline beside Elliot's Beach, Besant Nagar Ashtalakshmi Kovil (Arulmigu Sri Ashtalakshmi Temple) is a Hindu temple dedicated to the **eight forms of Goddess Lakshmi**, each representing a different form of prosperity and well-being. Completed in 1976, it has become one of Chennai's best-known modern temples, recognized for its distinctive multi-level design and scenic seaside setting. ([Wikipedia][1])

### What makes it special

Unlike most temples with a single main sanctum, this temple is arranged across **multiple levels**, allowing devotees to visit separate shrines dedicated to **Adi, Dhana, Dhanya, Gaja, Santana, Dhairya (Veera), Vijaya, and Vidya Lakshmi** in a continuous route. The layout is designed so pilgrims can complete the circuit without crossing over sanctum spaces, making both the architecture and the worship experience distinctive. ([Wikipedia][1])

### Architecture and setting

The temple combines **modern construction with traditional South Indian Hindu architecture**. Built facing the Bay of Bengal, it features colorful towers, sculpted deities, and elevated sanctums connected by stairways. Its location beside Elliot's Beach gives visitors sea breezes and, from the upper levels, views toward the shoreline, making it a popular destination for both worship and sightseeing. ([Karthi Travels][2])

### Religious and cultural significance

The temple is dedicated to the concept of **Ashta Lakshmi**, the eight manifestations of Lakshmi associated with wealth, courage, knowledge, food, victory, prosperity, children, and spiritual well-being. Alongside the Lakshmi shrines, the complex also includes shrines associated with Vishnu and other Hindu deities, reflecting its Vaishnava tradition. Since its consecration in 1976, it has become an important spiritual landmark in Chennai and attracts especially large crowds on Fridays and during Lakshmi-related festivals. ([HRCE][3])
